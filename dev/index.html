ProximalSolvers.jl

Solvers for problems of the form

\[\underset{x}{\text{minimize}} \quad F(x) = f(x) + g(x),\]

with a loss $f$ and a penalty or constraint $g$: the lasso and its relatives, sparse generalized linear models, the group lasso, nonconvex penalties, low-rank matrix regression, the graphical lasso and other problems of high-dimensional statistics. The functions $f$ and $g$ come from ProximalBase.jl, which supplies their values, gradients and proximal operators.

The core is coordinate descent, for a penalty that separates across coordinates or groups of coordinates. On top of it come penalty paths with screening, reweighting, exact refits on a support, and proximal Newton for generalized linear models. For a penalty that does not separate, such as the nuclear norm, there are proximal gradient methods and ADMM.

Installation

using Pkg
Pkg.add("ProximalSolvers")

ProximalSolvers requires Julia 1.10 or later. It was called ProximalOPT before its first release.

A first example

The lasso, $\|y - X\beta\|^2/(2n) + \lambda\|\beta\|_1$, by coordinate descent, at one penalty level and along a path:

using ProximalBase, ProximalSolvers, Random

rng = Xoshiro(1)
n, p = 200, 1000
X = randn(rng, n, p)
y = X[:, 1:5] * [2.0, -1.5, 1.0, -1.0, 0.5] + randn(rng, n)

f = (1 / n) * LeastSquaresLoss(y, X)            # ‖y - Xβ‖² / (2n)
λmax = lambda_max(f, NormL1(1.0), SparseIterate(p))
β = SparseIterate(p)                            # the starting point, overwritten
res = solve!(β, f, NormL1(0.1λmax), CDSolver())
findall(!iszero, β)

Every solver is called the same way, solve!(x, f, g, solver), and overwrites the starting point x with the solution.

Coordinate descent is usually the fastest when it applies: it exploits the structure of the loss to make each coordinate step cost $O(n)$ or less, and the sparsity of the solution to skip most coordinates. The drivers built on it compute

  • the solutions along a decreasing sequence of penalty levels, screened by the strong rule (solve_path, see Penalty paths and screening);
  • estimators that alternate solves with updates of the penalty, such as the scaled lasso (reweight!), and refits on a support (refit!), see Reweighting and refitting.

The documentation

  • Calling the solvers covers the calling convention, the options, the results and the stopping rules.
  • The method pages state each algorithm as implemented, with the derivations of its steps and stopping rules: Proximal gradient methods, Active-set proximal gradient, ADMM, and for coordinate descent The method, The losses, Penalty paths and screening, Reweighting and refitting and Proximal Newton.
  • A model of your own shows how to add a loss to coordinate descent.
  • The examples are thirteen complete programs, each checking its result. The same scripts are in the examples/ folder of the repository, and the test suite runs them.

The functions, their proximal operators and the sparse iterates are documented with ProximalBase, at mlakolar.github.io/ProximalBase.jl.

Related packages

ProximalSolvers is one of five Julia packages for high-dimensional statistics, three for estimation and inference and two for the optimization beneath them:

Package
CoordinateDescent.jlthe lasso and its paths; the group, square-root, scaled and heteroscedastic lasso; varying-coefficient models
CovarianceSelection.jlGaussian graphical models: the graphical lasso, CLIME and ACLIME, precision matrices by the scaled lasso, the fused graphical lasso, and the difference of two precision matrices
DiffPrecTest.jlconfidence intervals and tests for the difference of two precision matrices
ProximalSolvers.jl (this package)the solvers: coordinate descent with penalty paths, screening and active sets; proximal gradient methods, proximal Newton and ADMM
ProximalBase.jlthe building blocks: losses, penalties and constraints with their gradients and proximal operators, and sparse iterates; documentation with the mathematics

Most users need one of the first three. To solve a problem of your own, use ProximalSolvers; to write your own algorithm, ProximalBase.

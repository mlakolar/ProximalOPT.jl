Penalty paths and screening

A penalty with a level $\lambda$ defines a family of problems, and the solution is usually wanted along a decreasing sequence of levels, to choose $\lambda$ by cross-validation or an information criterion. solve_path computes it with warm starts and screening, and solve!(x, f, g, CDSolver(); continuation) uses the same machinery to reach one level.

Penalty families

These penalties of ProximalBase have a level, and solve_path(f, g, λs) replaces the level of g by each $\lambda$:

Penalty$g_\lambda(x)$weight of coordinate $k$
NormL1(λ, w)$\lambda\sum_k w_k\lvert x_k\rvert$$w_k$
NormL1Nonneg(λ, w)$\lambda\sum_k w_k x_k$ on $x \ge 0$$w_k$
ElasticNet(λ, λ₂, w)$\lambda\sum_k w_k\lvert x_k\rvert + \tfrac{\lambda_2}{2}\lVert x\rVert^2$$w_k$
Scaled(ElasticNet(α, 1 - α, w), λ)$\lambda\big(\alpha\sum_k w_k\lvert x_k\rvert + \tfrac{1 - \alpha}{2}\lVert x\rVert^2\big)$$\alpha w_k$
MCP(λ, a), SCAD(λ, a)nonconvex, with slope $\lambda$ at zero$1$
GroupNormL2(λ, groups, w)$\lambda\sum_k w_k\lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2$$w_k$

In each, a coordinate at zero is optimal (stationary, for MCP and SCAD) while the gradient of the loss over it is at most $\lambda w_k$ in absolute value, or in Euclidean norm for a group: the right derivative of every penalty at zero is $\lambda w_k$. A coordinate with $w_k = 0$ is not penalized.

The level of ElasticNet(λ, λ₂) is $\lambda$, with $\lambda_2$ fixed. The elastic-net path of glmnet at a fixed mixing $\alpha$ moves both terms with $\lambda$: it is the path of Scaled(ElasticNet(α, 1 - α), λ), whose level is the scale $\lambda$.

The largest useful level

Zero minimizes $f + g_\lambda$ exactly when every penalized coordinate satisfies this condition at zero:

\[\lambda_{\max} = \max_{k:\, w_k > 0} \frac{|\nabla_k f(0)|}{w_k} \qquad\Big(\max_k \frac{\|\nabla_{G_k} f(0)\|_2}{w_k} \text{ for groups}\Big).\]

lambda_max(f, g, x) computes it from the gradient at zero, for iterates like x; for the lasso of $\|y - X\beta\|^2/(2n)$ it is $\|X^\top y\|_\infty/n$. With unpenalized coordinates the formula still uses the gradient at zero, while the smallest level with all penalized coordinates at zero would use the gradient at the fit of the unpenalized ones. A common grid is geometric, from $\lambda_{\max}$ down to $\epsilon\lambda_{\max}$ with $\epsilon$ between $10^{-3}$ and $10^{-1}$.

Warm starts and the strong rule

Each level starts from the solution at the level before, which is close when the levels are close. Screening also uses it to predict which coordinates stay at zero. Let $c_j(\lambda) = \nabla_j f(\hat x(\lambda))$ at the solution $\hat x(\lambda)$. The sequential strong rule of Tibshirani et al. (2012) leaves coordinate $j$ out at the level $\lambda_k$ when

\[|c_j(\lambda_{k-1})| < (2\lambda_k - \lambda_{k-1})\, w_j .\]

The rule is exact if $|c_j(\lambda) - c_j(\lambda')| \le w_j|\lambda - \lambda'|$ along the path: then $|c_j(\lambda_k)| < (2\lambda_k - \lambda_{k-1})w_j + (\lambda_{k-1} - \lambda_k)w_j = \lambda_k w_j$, and coordinate $j$ is zero at $\lambda_k$. The condition often holds but can fail, so the rule is checked. At each level,

  1. the strong set $S$ holds the coordinates that pass the rule's threshold, $|c_j(\lambda_{k-1})| \ge (2\lambda_k - \lambda_{k-1})w_j$, those that are nonzero, and the unpenalized ones;
  2. coordinate descent solves the problem with its full passes over $S$ instead of all coordinates;
  3. one full gradient checks the optimality condition $|\nabla_j f(x)| \le \lambda_k w_j$ of every coordinate outside $S$; the violators join $S$, and step 2 runs again from the current solution.

The gradient computed in step 3 is the one the rule needs at the next level. solve_path starts the rule at the $\lambda_{\max}$ of its starting point, which need not be zero. The results report, per level, the passes and the number of checks: one when the strong rule made no mistake.

Calling solve_path

solve_path(f, g, λs, method = CDSolver(); x, screening = true, maxnnz = Inf, callback = nothing) takes decreasing nonnegative levels. The iterate x is the starting point and holds the last solution on return; it defaults to a zero SparseIterate when the loss has columns, and must be given otherwise, as an AtomIterate for a group penalty or a SymmetricSparseIterate for a symmetric matrix. The path stops early after the first solution with more than maxnnz nonzero coordinates, or when callback(k, λ, x, m) returns true; m is the model of the loss, current for x, from which a callback can compute a validation error, residuals or a gradient. The PathResults hold, per level, the level, the solution as a sparse vector (a sparse matrix for a matrix iterate), the objective, the passes, whether coordinate descent converged, and the number of checks.

Continuation

solve!(x, f, g, CDSolver(); continuation = K) sets x to zero and solves at the $K + 1$ levels

\[\lambda_j = \lambda_{\max}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\max}}\Big)^{j/K}, \qquad j = 0, 1, \dots, K,\]

geometrically from $\lambda_{\max}$ down to the level $\lambda$ of g, each warm started from the one before and screened as above (screening = true, the default except on a SymmetricSparseIterate, where a coordinate step changes the gradient everywhere, so that the checks cost more passes than they save). The levels before g only provide warm starts, so they are solved to the looser tolerance continuation_tol, $\sqrt{\mathrm{tol}}$ by default, and only g itself to tol; continuation_tol = tol solves every level fully. It returns the solution at g. When the level of g is zero or at least $\lambda_{\max}$, or g has no level (IndBox, ZeroFunction), it solves from zero directly.

On a convex problem the minimizer is the same either way, and continuation is a question of speed. On the lasso design of A lasso path, it took half the passes of a direct solve from zero, or fewer, at $0.01\lambda_{\max}$, and fewer at $0.02\lambda_{\max}$, but more at $0.05\lambda_{\max}$ and above, where the direct solve is short anyway.

For MCP and SCAD it also changes the answer. From zero, coordinate descent can stop at a poor stationary point when $p \gg n$ and the columns are correlated; along the levels, the solution follows a branch of stationary points from the zero solution, as along a path. In Other penalties, with MCP, it ends at less than half the objective of the direct solve, with a far smaller estimation error.

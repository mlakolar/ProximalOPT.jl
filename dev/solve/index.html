Calling the solvers

The call

Every solver minimizes $f(x) + g(x)$ through solve!:

res = solve!(x, f, g, solver)            # CDSolver, ProxNewton
res = solve!(x, f, g, solver, options)   # the proximal gradient solvers and ADMM

x is the starting point, and solve! overwrites it with the solution. The loss f and the penalty g are functions of ProximalBase. Each solver asks for some of their oracles, and for an iterate of a certain kind:

Solver$f$$g$x
ProximalGradientDescent, AccProxGradDescentsmooth: issmooth(f)proximable: isproximable(g)any array that f and g take, a SparseIterate, or an AtomIterate with the groups of a GroupNormL2
ActiveAccProxGradDescentsmooth, with restrictseparable or GroupNormL2, with restrict and kkt_violationa vector, a SparseIterate or an AtomIterate
ADMMSolverproximableproximablean Array
CDSolvera loss with a coordinate descent model, see Losses for coordinate descentseparable, or GroupNormL2a SparseIterate, a SymmetricSparseIterate, an AtomIterate for a group penalty, or a vector
ProxNewtona GLMLossseparablea SparseIterate

The iterates are those of ProximalBase: a SparseIterate stores the entries that have been nonzero and gives $O(1)$ access to any entry; a SymmetricSparseIterate is a sparse symmetric matrix whose coordinates are the entries of its lower triangle; an AtomIterate is a vector whose coordinates are groups of entries. A solver that takes a dense vector for coordinate descent or the active-set method converts it to one of these, and copies the solution back.

The solvers compute in the element type of x, Float64 or Float32. In Float32, tolerances below its precision, eps(Float32) ≈ 1.2e-7, cannot be met.

Options

ProximalOptions holds the iteration and call limits, the tolerances and the tracing of the proximal gradient solvers, which pass them on to each inner solve of the active-set method:

KeywordDefaultMeaning
maxiter200the largest number of iterations
ftol1e-6relative change of the objective
xtol1e-8relative change of the iterate
gradtol1e-5the optimality residual of FASTA
allow_f_increasesfalsekeep going when the objective increases
time_limitInfthe longest run time, in seconds
f_calls_limit, grad_calls_limit, prox_calls_limit0the largest number of evaluations of $f$, of its gradient and of proximal operators; 0 is no limit
store_trace, show_trace, extended_trace, printEveryfalse, false, false, 1record or print the progress
callbacknothingcalled every printEvery iterations; returning true stops

The tests of ftol, xtol and gradtol are those of Proximal gradient methods; the solver stops when any of them passes. ADMMSolver has its own tolerances and uses only maxiter, time_limit, prox_calls_limit, show_trace and printEvery. CDSolver and ProxNewton take their settings as keywords of their constructors instead: CDSolver(; maxpasses, tol, order, relative, anderson, momentum) and ProxNewton(; inner, maxiter, tol, β).

The call limits are checked after each iteration, which always finishes, so a count can exceed its limit by the evaluations of one iteration.

Traces and callbacks

With store_trace = true the proximal gradient solvers record an OptimizationState per iteration in res.trace: the iteration, the objective fg_x, the relative change of the iterate dxx and the inverse step size L. extended_trace = true also stores the iterate and the gradient. show_trace = true prints the same every printEvery iterations. A callback receives the trace, or the current state when the trace is not stored, and stops the solver by returning true.

For the penalty paths of coordinate descent, solve_path(...; callback) calls callback(k, λ, x, m) after each level instead; see Penalty paths and screening.
